How to Prepare Your Subfloor for Kitchen Flooring Installation

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

Step 1: Remove Existing Flooring and Clear Debris. Carefully pull up all old flooring materials, making sure to remove any staples, nails, or adhesive residue. The goal is a completely clean, bare subfloor. This ensures no lumps or bumps interfere with the new installation.

Step 2: Inspect for Damage, Unevenness, and Stability. Thoroughly examine the subfloor for any signs of damage like cracks, rot, or loose sections. Check for uneven spots – even slight dips or humps can lead to issues with your new floor. Ensure the subfloor is structurally sound and doesn't creak or flex.

Step 3: Level the Subfloor as Needed. If you find uneven areas, leveling is essential. For minor dips, self-leveling compounds work wonders. For more significant issues, additional patching or sanding might be required. This step prevents hollow spots, cracked tiles, or gaps in your new floor.

Step 4: Install Appropriate Underlayment or Moisture Barrier. Depending on your chosen flooring type (e.g., LVP, tile, hardwood), a specific underlayment or moisture barrier is crucial. This protects your new floor from moisture, provides sound insulation, and helps with comfort. Make sure to leave the recommended expansion gaps around the perimeter.

Proper subfloor prep is a task where professional expertise truly shines, especially with complex materials like tile or if your subfloor has significant issues.
