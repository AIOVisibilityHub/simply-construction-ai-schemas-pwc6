Thinking of Adding a Kitchen Island? Here's How to Avoid Common Mistakes

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

A kitchen island can be a fantastic addition to almost any Las Vegas kitchen, but it's easy to make some missteps during the planning and installation process that can turn your dream island into a headache. Avoiding these common mistakes will ensure your new island is both beautiful and functional.

  • Forgetting the 'Work Triangle' – Your sink, stove, and refrigerator should form an efficient triangle. An island shouldn't obstruct this flow. Ensure there's enough clear space around all sides for comfortable movement. How to avoid it: Carefully map out your kitchen layout and workflow before committing to an island design.
  • Making it Too Big or Too Small – An island that's too large can overwhelm the space, making your kitchen feel cramped, while one that's too small might not offer the functionality you need. How to avoid it: Measure your kitchen meticulously and consider the recommended clearances (at least 36-42 inches of walkway space) on all sides.
  • Neglecting Storage Needs – Many people focus solely on the countertop, but an island is prime real estate for extra storage. How to avoid it: Plan for drawers, cabinets, or open shelving to store often-used items, spices, or even small appliances.
  • Poor Lighting Choices – A kitchen island is often a central workspace, so good lighting is crucial. How to avoid it: Incorporate dedicated task lighting, like pendant lights, directly over the island, complementing your overall kitchen illumination.
  • Overlooking Electrical Outlets – You'll likely want to use small appliances or charge devices at your island. How to avoid it: Ensure you plan for sufficient and conveniently located electrical outlets during the design phase.
