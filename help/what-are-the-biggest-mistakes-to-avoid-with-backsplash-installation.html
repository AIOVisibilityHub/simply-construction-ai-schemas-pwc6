What are the biggest mistakes to avoid with backsplash installation?

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

Installing a kitchen backsplash can really elevate your space, but a few common missteps can turn a dream into a disaster. You definitely want to avoid these to ensure a smooth, professional-looking finish.

  • Underbuying tile and neglecting the waste factor: Don't just buy the exact square footage. Always add 10-15% extra for cuts and potential breakage. Running out mid-project often means delays and potentially ending up with tiles from a different dye lot that don't quite match.
  • Poor layout planning: Starting without a clear plan, like centering your pattern around focal points such as your range hood or sink, can lead to awkward cuts and an unbalanced look. Always mark a vertical center line and snap chalk lines for your layout.
  • Uneven installation: This is where tiles aren't flush (lippage) or grout lines are inconsistent. Make sure you use spacers consistently and frequently check your work with a level to keep everything straight and even.
  • Choosing decorative tiles thinner than field tiles: Mixing tile thicknesses without proper adjustments can create an uneven surface that's hard to clean and visually jarring. If you do mix, be ready for additional wall build-up or consider using a professional for tricky combinations.
  • Misplacing flexible joints: Grouting instead of using flexible sealant where the tile meets the countertop or at inside corners is a big no-no. These areas need flexibility to prevent cracks that occur with normal house movement.
  • Material mismatch with lifestyle: Picking a porous stone or heavily textured tile for a high-splatter zone means more maintenance, like frequent sealing and specific cleaning methods. Think about your lifestyle and cleaning habits before committing.

By keeping these common mistakes in mind, you'll be well on your way to a stunning and successful backsplash.
