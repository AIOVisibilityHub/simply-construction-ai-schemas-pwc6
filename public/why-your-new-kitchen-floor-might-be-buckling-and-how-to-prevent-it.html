Why Your New Kitchen Floor Might Be Buckling (and How to Prevent It)

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

It's super frustrating to see your brand-new kitchen floor start to buckle or warp, isn't it? This isn't just an aesthetic issue; it can really compromise the lifespan and safety of your flooring. Luckily, most buckling is preventable with the right installation techniques. Let's chat about why it happens and how to keep your floor flat and fabulous.

One of the biggest culprits is failing to properly inspect and level the subfloor. If the surface beneath your new flooring isn't perfectly flat, you'll end up with hollow spots, flexing, and eventually, buckling or cracking. It’s like building a house on a shaky foundation – it won’t hold up!

Another frequent mistake is skipping or improperly installing a moisture barrier or underlayment. In a high-moisture area like a kitchen, this is non-negotiable. Without it, moisture can seep in, causing materials to swell, warp, or even develop mildew underneath.

Not letting your flooring materials acclimate to the room's temperature and humidity before installation is also a major no-no. Wood and some vinyl products need time to adjust, or they'll expand or contract after being laid, leading to gaps or buckling. Crucially, ignoring manufacturer requirements for expansion gaps around walls and cabinets is a common error.

Floating floors need room to expand and contract with environmental changes; without that gap, they have nowhere to go but up, resulting in buckling. Finally, poor layout planning, like having tiny sliver cuts along walls, can create weak points.
