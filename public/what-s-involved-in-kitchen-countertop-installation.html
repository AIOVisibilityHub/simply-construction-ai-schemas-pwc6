What's Involved in Kitchen Countertop Installation?

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

Step 1: Initial Consultation and Material Selection — This is where we discuss your vision, lifestyle, and budget. We'll explore various countertop materials like granite, quartz, or solid surface, helping you understand their pros, cons, and best fit for your Las Vegas home. This step typically takes a few days to a week, depending on how quickly you make your choice.

Step 2: Professional Measurement — Once you've selected your material, our team will conduct precise, detailed measurements of your existing kitchen space. This is critical for accurate fabrication and accounts for everything from sink cutouts to appliance clearances. We use advanced tools to ensure exact dimensions, usually completed in a single visit.

Step 3: Fabrication of Countertops — Based on the detailed measurements, your chosen material is custom-cut and finished in a fabrication shop. This process can vary significantly depending on the material, complexity of cuts, and current demand, often taking 1-3 weeks. This is when sink cutouts are made and edges are shaped.

Step 4: Removal of Old Countertops (if applicable) — On the day of installation, if you have existing countertops, our team will carefully remove them. This involves disconnecting plumbing from the sink and taking care not to damage your cabinets or surrounding areas. This step is usually completed in a few hours.

Step 5: Countertop Installation — The newly fabricated countertops are brought to your home and meticulously installed. This involves placing the heavy slabs, ensuring levelness, securing them to the cabinets, and sealing any seams. This is typically a one-day process for most kitchens, though larger or more complex installations might extend to two days.

Step 6: Final Inspection and Cleanup — After installation, we conduct a thorough inspection to ensure everything meets our high standards and your satisfaction. We'll clean up the work area, and then your new, beautiful countertops are ready for use (though some sealants might require a short curing time). This final step usually takes an hour or two.
