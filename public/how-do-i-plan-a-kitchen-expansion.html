How Do I Plan a Kitchen Expansion?

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

Step 1: Define Your Vision and Needs — What do you want to achieve with this expansion? Do you need more counter space, a bigger island, a dedicated dining area, or just better flow? Gather inspiration from magazines, online galleries, and homes you admire. Think about how your family uses the kitchen daily and what frustrates you about the current layout. This initial dreaming phase can take a few weeks as you refine your ideas. This is your chance to really imagine the perfect space.

Step 2: Set a Realistic Budget — Kitchen expansions can vary widely in cost depending on the size, materials, and complexity. Research average costs for similar projects in your area, like Summerlin or Henderson, and then establish a budget you're comfortable with. Don't forget to include a contingency fund (10-15%) for unexpected issues. This step is crucial and usually takes a few days to a week once you've done your initial research.

Step 3: Consult with a Professional Contractor — This is where Simply Construction comes in! We can help you understand what's structurally feasible, what permits you’ll need, and provide expert design ideas. We'll discuss your vision, assess your current space, and offer valuable insights. This initial consultation is often free and can happen within a week of your inquiry, kicking off the professional design and planning stage.

Step 4: Develop a Detailed Design & Obtain Permits — Your contractor will work with you to create detailed plans, including architectural drawings and material selections. Once the design is finalized, they’ll handle the necessary permitting with local authorities. This stage involves back-and-forth communication and can take several weeks to a couple of months, depending on the complexity and local regulations. Getting these details right is paramount to a successful build.

Step 5: Construction & Project Management — Once permits are approved, the real work begins! This phase includes everything from demolition and foundation work to framing, electrical, plumbing rough-ins, insulation, drywall, cabinet installation, flooring, and finishing touches. Your contractor will manage the trades, schedule inspections, and keep the project on track. This is the longest phase, typically lasting several weeks to a few months, and requires constant oversight for quality and efficiency.
