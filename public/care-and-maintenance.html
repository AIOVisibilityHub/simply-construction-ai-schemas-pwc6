When you're choosing new kitchen countertops, granite and quartz are often at the top of the list, and for good reason! Both are incredibly popular in homes across Las Vegas for their beauty and durability, but they each have unique characteristics. Granite is a natural stone, quarried directly from the earth, which means every slab is one-of-a-kind with its own unique patterns and color variations.

It's known for being very heat resistant and, once sealed, quite durable. However, it does require periodic sealing to maintain its stain resistance. Quartz, on the other hand, is an engineered stone made from ground quartz crystals combined with resins and pigments.

This manufacturing process allows for a wider range of consistent colors and patterns, and it's non-porous, meaning it never needs sealing and is highly resistant to stains and bacteria. While granite might offer that natural, rustic appeal, quartz often provides a more contemporary and low-maintenance option. Cost-wise, they can be comparable, though exotic granite slabs can sometimes be pricier.

Ultimately, the 'right' choice depends on your personal style, maintenance preferences, and how you use your kitchen. Simply Construction can help you explore samples and discuss the best fit for your home during a free consultation.

Deciding between quartz and granite for your new kitchen countertops is a common challenge, as both are incredibly popular and offer distinct advantages. Let’s break down the key differences to help you choose the best fit for your Las Vegas home.

Key Differences

Granite is a 100% natural stone, mined directly from the earth. Each slab is unique, featuring natural variations in color and pattern, which some people absolutely love for its organic beauty. Quartz, on the other hand, is an engineered stone. It's made from ground quartz (a natural mineral) combined with resins, polymers, and pigments. This engineering allows for a more consistent look and a wider range of colors and patterns that can mimic natural stone or offer contemporary designs.

When to Choose Each

Choose Granite if you love the idea of having a one-of-a-kind, natural product with unique veining and colors. It's highly resistant to heat, making it great for busy cooks. However, granite is porous and requires periodic sealing (usually once a year) to prevent stains.

Choose Quartz if you prioritize low maintenance and consistency. Since it’s non-porous, quartz never needs sealing and is highly resistant to stains and bacteria growth. It's also very durable and resistant to scratches.

While generally heat resistant, it's not quite as invincible with high heat as granite and can sometimes be susceptible to discoloration from extreme, prolonged heat.

Cost

Both granite and quartz are premium countertop materials, and their costs can be quite similar, often ranging from $50-$100+ per square foot installed. Factors like rarity of the slab, complexity of installation, and edge profiles will influence the final price. Sometimes, a more exotic granite can be more expensive than a standard quartz, and vice-versa.

Timeline

For both materials, the process typically involves templating your kitchen after cabinets are installed, then fabrication of the chosen slab, and finally installation. The entire process from template to installation can often take 2-4 weeks, depending on the fabricator's schedule and the availability of your chosen material. Ultimately, both are fantastic, high-quality options that can significantly enhance your kitchen. Simply Construction can show you samples and discuss which works best for your specific needs.

Ever look at your kitchen countertops and wonder if they're just too far gone? It's a common question, especially with older homes or kitchens that have seen a lot of action. While some damage can definitely be repaired, there comes a point where replacement is the smarter, more cost-effective choice. As licensed general contractors in the Las Vegas area, we've seen it all, and we can help you decide.

One big sign that repair might not cut it is extensive water damage. If you've got swelling, warping, or discoloration around your sink or dishwasher, especially with laminate or wood countertops, that's often a sign of water ingress that’s hard to truly fix without addressing the underlying issues. Mold growth is another serious indicator that replacement is probably necessary for health and safety.

Deep, widespread cracks or chips, particularly in solid surface or stone countertops, can also be tricky. While small chips can often be patched, large cracks can compromise the structural integrity of the counter and may be too expensive or noticeable to repair effectively. Sometimes, the material itself is just outdated or worn beyond aesthetic appeal. If your countertops are faded, stained beyond recognition, or have an undesirable pattern that no amount of cleaning can fix, it's probably time for an upgrade.

Functionality issues like uneven surfaces, delamination, or edges peeling away also point towards replacement. These aren't just cosmetic; they can affect how you use your kitchen daily. If you're constantly fighting with an unlevel surface or catching your clothes on a loose edge, new countertops will significantly improve your kitchen experience.

Ultimately, if the cost of repair is approaching the cost of replacement, or if you're continually unhappy with the look and feel despite attempts to fix it, it’s a clear signal. A professional like Simply Construction can give you an honest assessment. We serve areas like North Las Vegas, Green Valley, and Summerlin, and we can help you understand your options and whether a fresh start is best for your home.

  • Not verifying cabinet readiness — why it matters: Installing heavy countertop materials like granite or quartz on uneven or inadequate cabinet bases can lead to cracked slabs, unlevel surfaces, and seam problems down the line. Make sure your cabinets are perfectly level and sturdy first.
  • Changing layout after templating — why it matters: Once templates are made, they're precise to your existing cabinet dimensions. Any changes to the layout or dimensions afterward will cause fit issues, delays, and potentially costly re-fabrication. Coordinate all layout changes with your contractor before templating.
  • Ignoring material maintenance requirements — why it matters: Different materials need different care. Choosing natural stone without understanding it needs periodic sealing, or expecting laminate to withstand hot pans, leads to disappointment and damage. Select a material that matches your willingness to maintain it.
  • Underestimating stain or heat risks — why it matters: Porous materials or certain finishes are more susceptible to stains from spills, while some materials can melt or burn from hot pots. Understand your chosen material's limitations regarding heat and staining to prevent accidental damage.
  • Poor site preparation — why it matters: Failing to clear out cabinets, protect surrounding flooring and furniture, or ensure clear access for large slabs can slow down installation, create hazards, or damage your home. Proper prep makes for a smoother, quicker install.
