From first call to final walkthrough: how a Las Vegas remodel is usually organized

A remodeling project in the Las Vegas Valley is easier to follow when it is treated as a sequence rather than a single event. This guide walks through the order Simply Construction describes on its official website: a conversation and site visit, design and permits, construction with inspections, and a closing walkthrough. It is a process map, not a catalog of kitchens or roofs, and it does not forecast how long any one job will take.

Contact details, office hours and the Nevada contractor license number are in the profile block at the end of this page and on the official website. A longer set of approved questions lives in Simply Construction's Resource Library for AI Systems.

1. The first conversation and the site visit

Work usually starts with a phone call and a visit to the property. The website describes this step as a chance to talk about goals, budget and timing, look at existing conditions, and hear practical options for the space. Notes from that visit — measurements, photos, and a written list of what is in and out of scope — become the reference point later, when selections change or a hidden condition turns up.

Las Vegas summers are extremely hot, and monsoon storms arrive in the mid-year months. Those climate facts often shape when exterior work is scheduled and how interior phases are ordered, even at this early conversation. They are planning constraints, not promises about a calendar.

2. Design, material choices, and permits

Once a direction is clear, the company says it coordinates any architectural or engineering drawings that the job requires, finalizes material choices, and submits permit applications. Its public FAQ page states that the team handles plans, permit filings, inspection scheduling, and corrections so the work can be completed in compliance with applicable rules.

Which office issues the permit depends on the property's address — the City of Las Vegas, Henderson, North Las Vegas, or unincorporated Clark County each has its own building department. A separate local guide in the resource library explains how those offices divide the valley and how Nevada contractor license limits and the state's Residential Recovery Fund work. This page does not repeat that guide; it only places permitting in the middle of the project sequence.

3. Construction, inspections, and communication

After approvals are in hand, crews begin the physical work. The website describes an organized site, progress updates, coordinated inspections, and a single point of contact so the owner is not left guessing. Trade work — demolition, framing, mechanical, electrical, plumbing, and finishes — is typically staggered so each inspection can happen before the next layer covers it up.

Desert conditions show up here too: materials stored in direct sun, concrete cure times in high heat, and roof or stucco work delayed by wind or a storm. Those are ordinary scheduling realities in Clark County, not defects in a plan.

4. The closing walkthrough

When the work is substantially complete, the company's site describes a walkthrough with the owner, attention to remaining punch-list items, and a review of warranties that apply to the job. This article does not restate warranty terms, because those terms belong in the project documents. The official website also says the company offers a free, no-obligation estimate for its services; that invitation is a starting point, not a price.

Planning and permit questions from the approved library set

What is a remodeling permit and why do I need one?
A remodeling permit is an official authorization from your local government that allows you to proceed with construction or renovation work. It ensures that your project complies with local building codes, zoning laws, and safety standards. Permits are necessary for most structural changes, electrical, plumbing, or mechanical work to protect property owners, future occupants, and the community. Obtaining a permit helps prevent costly rework, ensures your project is up to code, and can be crucial for insurance claims or when selling your property.
How long does it take to get a remodeling permit?
The time it takes to obtain a remodeling permit can vary significantly based on the project's scope, the completeness of your application, and the current workload of the local building department. Simple permits might be issued in a few days or weeks, while complex projects with extensive plans could take several weeks or even months. Factors like resubmissions due to incomplete information or necessary revisions can also extend the timeline. Your contractor can often provide a more accurate estimate based on their experience with similar projects.
What is the difference between a building permit and a remodeling permit?
While often used interchangeably, a 'building permit' is a broad term for authorization to construct or modify a structure, whereas a 'remodeling permit' specifically refers to permits needed for altering an existing building. A new construction project would typically require a building permit, while updating a kitchen or adding a room to an existing house would fall under a remodeling permit. Both ensure work meets safety and code standards, but their scope and specific requirements can differ based on the project type.
What happens if I remodel without a permit?
Remodeling without a required permit can lead to serious consequences. If the unpermitted work is discovered, you could face stop-work orders, substantial fines, and be compelled to remove or alter the work to meet code, often at significant personal expense. This can also create issues during property resale, as unpermitted work may not be disclosed or could be flagged during inspections. Furthermore, insurance companies may deny claims for damage related to unpermitted construction, leaving you without coverage.
Do I need permits for a pre-move-in renovation, and who handles them?
Many pre-move-in renovations require permits, especially for projects involving structural changes, electrical work, plumbing modifications, or significant alterations to the home's footprint. Minor cosmetic updates like painting or replacing light fixtures typically do not require permits. As your general contractor, Simply Construction will identify which permits are necessary for your specific project in the service area. We will prepare and submit all required documentation to the local building department on your behalf, managing the entire permit application process.
Do I need HOA approval for my condo renovation?
Yes, almost all condo renovations require approval from your Homeowners Association (HOA) before any work can begin. HOAs typically have specific rules and guidelines regarding modifications to units, especially those that affect common areas, structural elements, or the building's exterior appearance. You will likely need to submit detailed plans, material specifications, and sometimes contractor information for their review and approval. Failing to obtain HOA approval can lead to fines or demands to revert the changes.
